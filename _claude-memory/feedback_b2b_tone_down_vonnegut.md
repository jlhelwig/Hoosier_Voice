For pieces aimed at compliance officers, CISOs, engineering leadership, security buyers, procurement, or any reader about to commit org resources to a product or program — **proactively warn Jeff that the full Vonnegut voice should be dialed back.**

**Why:** Jeff confirmed (2026-05-06): "Yes, warn me for b2b to tone it down." Self-deprecating undercuts ("...maybe with a grim smile") read as unserious to someone about to put their name on a budget line. The risk is real and Jeff wants the heads-up before he writes, so he can choose deliberately rather than discover the mismatch on a re-read.

**How to apply:** When Jeff brings a draft, a kernel, or a working title and the target audience skews enterprise / decision-maker / authority-required, flag at the top of structural review:
- **Keep** the Vonnegut bones — plain words, no hedging, no AI-slop tells, fragments that earn it, conversational sentence shapes
- **Tone down** the showy tics — "...wow", "...maybe", deadpan undercuts on weighty claims, self-deprecating asides where the audience reads humor as evasion
- **Preserve** trust signals — let claims land at face value; don't undercut authority where authority is the product

**B2B audience triggers in this project:**
- Jiminy / governance pieces (compliance, CISO)
- Procurement-shaped product pitches
- Authority-claim pieces ("here's why X changes everything") aimed at C-level
- Anything explicitly aimed at engineering leadership for a budget decision

**NOT B2B (full Vonnegut is fine):**
- Personal narrative pieces (ADHD, Project Manager)
- Pattern essays for builders/PMs
- Engineer-deep technical pieces — engineers love Vonnegut, no toning needed
