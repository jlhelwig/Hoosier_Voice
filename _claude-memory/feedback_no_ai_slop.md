Jeff's voice has a name and a concrete reference. **Hoosier style, Vonnegut-shaped.**

Jeff said directly: "Kurt Vonnegut was a famous Hoosier writer, and my writing sounds a bit like him." This is the most useful single voice anchor — Vonnegut is specific enough to imitate and well-documented enough to study.

**Vonnegut tells visible in Jeff's existing prose** (ADHD piece, 2026-05-06):
- Flat declarative opener that's quietly devastating: "Claude hurt my feelings last week."
- Self-deprecating undercut aside: "...maybe with a grim smile"
- Deadpan delivery on absurd reveal: "Yes, I was making a prosthetic... wow."
- Fragment-as-comic-timing: "Who is a first grade teacher."
- Willingness to start sentences with "And" or "But"
- Plain words over Latinate ones; no academic vocabulary
- Comma splices used deliberately
- Short paragraphs, often single-sentence
- Dark observations stated flatly, never milked

**Rule of thumb when editing:** read your proposed change against a page of Slaughterhouse-Five or Cat's Cradle. If the change wouldn't survive on Vonnegut's page, it doesn't survive on Jeff's.

**Hoosier is NOT Southern / Appalachian.** Indiana is Midwest. Avoid Southern/rural-South idioms — they are not Jeff's voice and reading them in his prose breaks the spell. Specifically NOT Hoosier: "holler" (Appalachian), "y'all" (Southern), "fixin' to" (Southern), "reckon" (Appalachian/British rural). Jeff caught "holler" on 2026-05-06 and corrected explicitly. Hoosier register is plain Midwestern modesty — closer to "let me know when..." or "give me a shout" than anything country-folksy.

**Why:** Jeff said directly, "I want this to sound human, not ai slop — no offense" and "I will rewrite with my typical Hoosier style of speaking." His voice is ragged on purpose. That texture is the point. Polishing it out makes it generic.

**How to apply:** During line edits, fix actual errors (typos, run-ons that hurt comprehension, capitalization inconsistencies) but **preserve voice tics**. Do not normalize sentence length. Do not add tidy summary sentences at paragraph ends. Do not parallelize for symmetry.

Specific AI-slop tells to avoid in suggestions or rewrites:
- Em-dash parallels ("not just X — it's Y", "X. Y. Z.")
- Three-part lists with matched verbs
- Hedging adverbs (essentially, fundamentally, surprisingly, importantly) — Vonnegut also disliked literary -ly adverbs as crutches
- "Here's the thing," "The truth is," "Imagine if"
- Latinate where Anglo-Saxon works (utilize → use, leverage → use)
- Abstract nouns standing in for concrete images
- Summary sentence at end of every paragraph
- Adjective stacks (compelling, fascinating, revolutionary, transformative)
- Symmetrical clause rhythm

When in doubt: read the sentence aloud. If it sounds like a TED-talk transcript, cut it.
